Case Study: Cyberbullying – When Screens Become Weapons


What is Cyberbullying?


Cyberbullying is the repeated and deliberate use of digital technology to harass, humiliate, threaten, or embarrass another person. It often feels more painful than face-to-face bullying because it spreads quickly and never seems to stop.


How it typically happens?


It usually begins with a personal conflict — a breakup, fight, or jealousy. The bully creates a fake account or uses their own to post morphed photos, private chats, or abusive comments. The content is then shared in college WhatsApp groups, Instagram stories, and class forums. Within hours, others start liking, commenting, and forwarding it, turning one person’s anger into group harassment. The victim receives nonstop abusive messages, tags, and threats, leaving them feeling trapped and helpless.


Who is Usually Targeted?


College students aged 18–24, especially girls and students from minority communities, are the most common targets in India. Those who are active on social media with public or semi-public profiles become easy victims.


A Real-Life Like Example:


Priya Sharma, a cheerful 20-year-old second-year engineering student from Bhopal, broke up with her boyfriend in October 2025. Hurt and angry, he created a fake Instagram account and posted an edited explicit photo of her with vulgar captions and her real name and college tag. He shared the link in their batch WhatsApp group. In just two days, the post went viral among students. Priya started receiving hundreds of abusive DMs, slut-shaming comments, and threats. She stopped attending lectures, deleted all her social media accounts, and fell into severe anxiety and depression. Her grades dropped drastically, and she needed months of counseling to recover.


Consequences:


Cyberbullying can destroy mental health, academic performance, and self-confidence. In extreme cases, it has pushed students to drop out or attempt suicide.
Key Reflection:
One careless post or share can ruin someone’s life. Always think before you click.
